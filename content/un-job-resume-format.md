# UN Job Resume Format: A Complete Guide for Aspiring UN Professionals

**Looking to land a position at the United Nations?** Crafting a resume that aligns with UN standards is essential. This guide breaks down the UN job resume format, highlighting the key sections, formatting tips, and content strategies that will help your application stand out.

## Understand the UN Resume Structure

The UN prefers a concise, two-page document that showcases both technical expertise and soft skills. The typical structure includes:

- **Header** – Name, contact details, LinkedIn, and professional title.
- **Profile Summary** – A brief paragraph that captures your experience, core competencies, and career goals.
- **Core Competencies** – A bulleted list of relevant skills tailored to the job posting.
- **Professional Experience** – Chronological entries with measurable achievements.
- **Education & Certifications** – Degrees, institutions, and relevant certifications.
- **Languages & Cultural Competence** – Fluency levels and cross-cultural experience.
- **Volunteer & Service Experience** – Highlights of humanitarian or NGO work.

## Key Formatting Tips

- **Keep it to 2 pages** – UN recruiters review dozens of resumes; brevity is valued.
- **Use a clean, professional font** – Times New Roman or Arial, 10‑12 pt.
- **Avoid excessive graphics** – Stick to text; a simple header logo is acceptable.
- **Employ bullet points** – Each bullet should start with a strong action verb and include a quantifiable result when possible.
- **Use the UN’s preferred language** – English, French, or Spanish, depending on the position. English is the most common.

## Crafting Your Experience Section

Your experience section is where you demonstrate impact. Follow this formula:

1. **Job Title, Organization, Location, Dates**  
2. **Bullet 1:** Action verb + responsibility + measurable outcome.  
3. **Bullet 2:** Collaboration or cross-functional initiative.  
4. **Bullet 3:** Any awards or recognitions.

**Example:**

- *Program Coordinator, UNICEF, Nairobi, Kenya | Jan 2021‑Present*  
  - Designed and managed a $2 million child nutrition program, improving outcomes by 35% across 12 districts.  
  - Collaborated with local NGOs to train 500 community health workers, enhancing service delivery in rural areas.  
  - Received UNICEF’s “Excellence in Program Delivery” award in 2022.

## Highlighting Soft Skills and Cultural Competence

The UN values adaptability, intercultural communication, and ethical leadership. Showcase these through:

- **Language Proficiency:** List languages and proficiency levels (e.g., French – Advanced, Arabic – Conversational).  
- **Cross-Cultural Projects:** Briefly describe projects involving diverse teams.  
- **Ethical Decision-Making:** Mention any situations where you upheld integrity and transparency.

## Final Checklist Before Submission

- **Tailor to the Job Description:** Match keywords and required competencies.  
- **Proofread for Errors:** A single typo can cost you an interview.  
- **Include a Cover Letter:** Align it with the resume’s narrative.  
- **Save as PDF:** Preserve formatting across devices.

## Conclusion

Mastering the UN job resume format is the first step toward a successful career with one of the world’s most respected organizations. By structuring your resume clearly, quantifying achievements, and emphasizing cross-cultural competence, you’ll present a compelling case to UN hiring teams.
