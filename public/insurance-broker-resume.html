Insurance Broker Resume: Crafting a Winning Document

Creating a standout insurance broker resume is essential to open doors in a competitive market. Your resume must showcase your expertise in underwriting, client relations, and sales while highlighting measurable achievements. By following a structured format, you’ll capture the attention of hiring managers and applicant‑tracking systems (ATS) alike.

1. Key Sections of an Insurance Broker Resume

A professional resume should include the following core sections:

  • Contact Information – Name, phone, email, LinkedIn, and location.
  • Professional Summary – 2‑3 sentences summarizing experience, specialty areas, and career goals.
  • Core Competencies – A concise list of skills (e.g., risk assessment, policy underwriting, client acquisition).
  • Professional Experience – Reverse‑chronological bullet points with quantifiable results.
  • Education & Certifications – Degrees, licensing (e.g., CPCU, ARM), and relevant coursework.
  • Technical Proficiency – CRM tools, insurance software, and data analysis platforms.
  • Awards & Recognition – Any industry accolades or performance awards.

2. Highlighting Your Sales & Client Management Skills

Insurance brokers thrive on relationships. Use action verbs and numbers to demonstrate impact:

  • Generated $4 million in new business within 18 months by targeting high‑net‑worth clients.
  • Reduced client churn by 22% through proactive policy reviews and personalized communication.
  • Conducted risk‑analysis workshops for 50+ corporate clients, resulting in a 15% increase in policy uptake.

Include specific achievements that align with the company’s objectives, such as expanding into new markets or improving claim resolution times.

3. Tailoring Your Resume for ATS and Hiring Managers

Modern recruiters rely on ATS to filter candidates. Optimize your resume by:

  • Using industry‑specific keywords such as “underwriting,” “claims management,” “compliance,” and “risk mitigation.”
  • Formatting cleanly: avoid tables or images that ATS can’t read.
  • Choosing a standard font (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman) and a single‑column layout.
  • Incorporating measurable data: percentages, dollar amounts, and timeframes give context to your achievements.

By aligning your language with the job description, you increase the likelihood of passing the initial screening.

4. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Excessive jargon: Keep language clear and accessible.
  • Generic statements: Replace “responsible for sales” with specific results.
  • Long paragraphs: Use bullet points for readability.
  • Missing metrics: Numbers illustrate your contribution and set you apart.

Avoiding these pitfalls ensures your resume is professional, concise, and compelling.

Conclusion

An insurance broker resume that blends clear structure, quantifiable achievements, and keyword optimization will set you apart from the competition. Focus on the value you bring to clients and employers, and present it in a format that both humans and ATS can easily digest. With a polished resume, you’ll be one step closer to securing the role you desire.
